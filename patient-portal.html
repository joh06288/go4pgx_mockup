Resources

A patient-friendly guide to understanding your pharmacogenomics results and getting the most out of Go4PGx Everywhere.

1What is Pharmacogenomics?

Pharmacogenomics (PGx) is the science of how your genetic makeup affects how you respond to medications. Your DNA influences how quickly your body breaks down drugs, which can make a standard dose too strong, too weak, or even dangerous for you.

~90%
of people carry at least one variant that affects medication response
~50%
carry 5 or more variants that could necessitate a prescription change
Lifetime
PGx results are valid for life — test once, apply forever
2Understanding Your Results

Your gene results table shows your diplotype (your two gene variants), phenotype (what it means functionally), and activity score (a numeric measure of how active the gene enzyme is).

PhenotypeWhat it means
Normal MetabolizerStandard drug processing — usual doses apply
Intermediate MetabolizerSlower than normal — may need dose adjustment
Poor MetabolizerVery slow or no enzyme activity — standard doses may be harmful or ineffective
3Drug-Gene Interactions

A drug-gene interaction alert means one of your medications is processed by a gene where you have a relevant variant. CPIC guidelines tell your provider how to act on this — whether to adjust the dose or choose an alternative drug.

4Sharing Your Results

Most pharmacies and clinics don't automatically have access to your PGx data. Sharing your results — via the QR code on your wallet card or a direct share link — gives your providers the information they need at the moment they're prescribing or dispensing.

5Your Privacy

Go4PGx Everywhere stores no personally identifiable information — no name, address, email, or phone number. Your only identifier is your username. You can revoke any sharing permission or delete your account entirely at any time from the Manage Account section.

I have a question about my CYP2C19 Poor Metabolizer result. My doctor wants to prescribe clopidogrel — what should I tell them?
Go4PGx Team · May 20, 2026 at 2:05 PM
Great question! CYP2C19 Poor Metabolizer status significantly reduces clopidogrel's effectiveness because your body can't convert it to its active form efficiently. We strongly recommend showing your provider the CDS alert on your PGx Results page — it links directly to the CPIC guideline for clopidogrel and CYP2C19. Alternatives like prasugrel or ticagrelor are not affected by CYP2C19. Your cardiologist can make the final call.
